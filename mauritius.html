Two million years

A shield, not a spike

Flow on flow of basalt spreads wide and low. At its greatest the volcano stood far higher than anything on the island today, with a crater at the summit.

The oldest exposed rocks are roughly 7.8 million years old. Ages vary between surveys.

Then it comes apart

Rain, sea and wind strip the cone away. What is left standing are the hard cores — and those are the mountains Mauritius actually has: Piton de la Petite Rivière Noire at 828 m, Pieter Both, Le Pouce, Le Morne Brabant.

The shape morphing under you is a real height model, interpolating from the young cone to the modern island.

Where everything happened

This is the real outline — 51 km east to west, 60 km north to south. The markers are the places the rest of the story needs.

Grand Port, where the Dutch sheltered in 1598. Port Louis, the Harbour of Tortoises. Le Morne, the maroon refuge. Aapravasi Ghat, where nearly half a million indentured workers landed.
